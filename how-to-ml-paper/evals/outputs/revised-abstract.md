Decoder-only transformers often execute every block for every token; we investigate whether token-dependent block skipping reduces inference latency while limiting accuracy loss. We introduce SkipGate, a small learned gate that uses the current hidden state to execute each transformer block or bypass it through the residual path. Fine-tuning optimizes task loss with a penalty on executed blocks. On one 7B model and one GPU type at batch size one, we evaluate MMLU, GSM8K, ARC-C, and HellaSwag across five fine-tuning seeds. SkipGate reduces median end-to-end latency, including gating overhead, from 42 to 29 ms/token (31%), while mean task accuracy decreases from 63.8% to 63.5%. At a matched average number of executed blocks, a static-depth baseline achieves 61.9% accuracy at 27 ms/token. Token-dependent skipping therefore retains more accuracy than static depth at a 2 ms/token latency cost in this evaluation. Paired uncertainty intervals are unavailable, so these results do not establish accuracy equivalence to dense inference.

- Novelty relative to prior adaptive-depth methods remains unverified.
- Gate ablations are needed to assess its contribution; removal of redundant reasoning is not an established mechanism.
- Generalization beyond the tested model, GPU, and batch size, as well as throughput and energy benefits, remains untested.
